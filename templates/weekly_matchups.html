How role-aware scoring works across positions

QB · 25% Role

QB1/QB2 status plus expected pass attempts, rushing attempts and snap share.

RB · 25% Role

RB1/RB2/RB3 status plus touches, opportunities, rush share and red-zone work.

WR · 25% Role

WR1/WR2/WR3 status plus targets, target share, routes, snaps and red-zone targets.

TE · 25% Role

TE1/TE2 status plus targets, target share, routes, snaps and red-zone involvement.

K · 40% Role

K1/K2 status plus field-goal and extra-point opportunities. Backup kickers are heavily discounted.

A depth-chart label is not a hard cap. When an injury, committee or personnel change creates starter-level usage, the role grade can rise automatically.

How the QB matchup score is calculated

25% QB Strength

Current production when available, otherwise the 2026 projection relative to other quarterbacks.

25% QB Role & Dropbacks

QB1/QB2 depth-chart status plus expected pass attempts, rushing attempts and snap share.

30% Pass-Defense Matchup

Opponent secondary, pass rush, yards per attempt, touchdown rate, interception rate and sack rate.

15% Pass Trenches

Offensive pass protection versus opponent pass-rush strength.

5% Rushing Upside

Projected quarterback rushing attempts add a small ceiling adjustment.

How Strength of Schedule affects Weekly Rank

92% Current-Week Matchup Score

The position-specific role-aware matchup model remains the main weekly signal.

8% Season Strength of Schedule

Position-adjusted 2026 schedule difficulty is used as a tiebreaker for QB, RB, WR and TE. Rank #1 is the easiest schedule.

The underlying RB 30/10/15/25/20 formula is unchanged. SOS changes Weekly Rank, not the matchup model itself.

How the RB matchup score is calculated

30% Run Trenches

Current offensive-line run-blocking projection compared directly with the opponent’s run-defense grade.

10% RB Strength

Current production when available, otherwise the 2026 projection and position-relative skill rank.

15% Run Defense

Opponent rushing EPA, yards per carry, stuff rate and explosive-run rate.

25% RB Role & Expected Volume

RB1/RB2/RB3 depth-chart order plus estimated touches/opportunities, rush share, opportunity share and red-zone role.

20% Receiving Role

Target share plus how many PPR points the opponent allows running backs through the passing game.

Depth-chart role is a starting point, not a hard label. Projected/current workload can move an RB2 upward when injuries or a true committee create starter-level opportunity.

How the WR/TE matchup score is calculated

25% Player Strength

Current production when available, otherwise preseason projection and position-relative skill rank.

25% Role & Expected Volume

WR1/WR2/WR3 or TE1/TE2 status plus targets, target share, routes, snaps and red-zone usage.

30% Coverage Matchup

Opponent secondary, likely defender and positional PPR efficiency allowed.

10% Scheme Fit

Player production against the opponent’s dominant man-or-zone tendency.

10% Pass Trenches

Offensive pass protection compared with opponent pass-rush strength.

A low-volume WR4 or TE2 should no longer become the top matchup solely because he faces a weak secondary.

How the K matchup score is calculated

30% Kicker Strength

Projected/current fantasy scoring relative to other kickers.

40% K1/K2 Role & Volume

Confirmed starting-kicker status plus field-goal and extra-point opportunities per game.

30% Kicking Opportunity

A drive-and-stall proxy combining how easily the opponent allows yardage with how often it suppresses touchdowns.

Kicker matchup data is naturally noisier than RB/WR/TE usage data, so the model intentionally gives the confirmed K1 role more weight.
